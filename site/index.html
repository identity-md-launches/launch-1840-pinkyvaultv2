Anyone who holds a token and wants to be believed when they say they'll keep holding it.

  • The one who launched it

    “The team's tokens stay put for the first week.”

  • The one who holds a lot

    “I'm not selling into this. Watch my wallet.”

  • The one who was paid in it

    “I got these for the post. I'm holding them for a month.”

How it works

  1. Put IMD on it

    Pick a token you hold, a limit and a deadline, and lock IMD behind one sentence: my wallet won't move more than this before then.

  2. Time runs out

    Anyone closes the promise. That fixes the exact block range it covered, start to finish.

  3. The swarm counts

    The contract pays the IdentityMD oracle 0.5 IMD out of the bond. A panel of agents sums every transfer out of that wallet over those blocks and signs the number.

  4. The number decides

    Within the limit: the bond goes home. Over it: a tenth goes to the first watcher who asked, and the rest is split between PINKY stakers and a burn.

A promise covers one wallet and one ordinary token (ERC-20; NFTs aren't supported yet), and nobody can pause or edit it once it's made. If the oracle can't answer after three tries, the bond is returned.

Before launch the IdentityMD swarm audited the contracts: one audit of the code as written, four specialist audits and a judge. What they found was fixed, and the list is public, as are the swarm's own record of the job and the code exactly as it was deployed. That is a review by AI agents, not an audit by an outside firm, so read the contracts before you trust them.

Questions people ask

Is this a locker?

No. A locker takes your tokens away. Pinky never touches them: they stay in your wallet and you can sell any minute. What you lock is IMD, and selling costs you that.

What counts as moving?

Every transfer of that token out of the wallet: a sale, a send to your other wallet, a deposit into staking or a pool. Tokens coming in don't count.

What if I sell from another wallet?

Then the promise doesn't see it. A promise covers one wallet, the one that made it. That's why the board says whose wallet it is and how much it holds.

Who decides whether I kept it?

The IdentityMD oracle. A panel of agents sums every transfer out of the wallet over the exact blocks the promise covered, and the contract only accepts a number the oracle has signed. Nobody here can overrule it.

What does it cost?

One oracle question, 0.5 IMD, taken from the bond when someone asks. Keep your word and the rest comes home. Plus gas on Robinhood Chain for your two signatures.

Can I cancel or change a promise?

No. Not you, not us. Once it's made, it runs until the deadline, and it keeps counting until someone closes it, so close it yourself when the time is up.

What if the oracle doesn't answer?

Anyone can ask again after a day, three times in all. After three failed tries, or a week after closing, the rest of the bond goes back to you.

Who can change the contracts?

Nobody can change the code. The vault's owner can point it at another oracle signer or Intake, which is a real trust point: the owner is 0x89d6…f8ef, the team's wallet. Staking has no owner at all.

Does it work for NFTs?

Not yet. A promise counts amounts, and an NFT transfer carries a token id instead. The form refuses an NFT address so nobody gets a promise that can't be judged.
